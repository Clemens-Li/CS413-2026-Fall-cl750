0:00--0:35 | Slide 1 -- Title
- Introduce the goal: compile a richer functional language into pure, untyped lambda calculus.

0:35--1:35 | Slide 2 -- What the project must accomplish
- Contrast the source language features with the three-form target language.
- Emphasize the central constraint: this is a compiler, not an evaluator that returns precomputed answers.
- State how success is checked: reduce compiled terms and observe results.

1:35--2:30 | Slide 3 -- Two languages, one pipeline
- Walk left to right through source AST, CompileVisitor, target AST, reducer, and observer.
- Mention the deliberate module separation: compiler, target operations, encodings, evaluator, observers, demos, and tests.

2:30--3:35 | Slide 4 -- Pure target terms and reduction
- Define Var, Lam, and App, then explain weak left-to-right CBV.
- Explain beta-value steps: evaluate the function, evaluate the argument, then substitute only when the argument is a lambda value.
- Clarify that budget exhaustion means more work was needed; it does not prove nontermination.

3:35--4:30 | Slide 5 -- Scope safety
- Use the capture-hazard example: substituting x := y into lambda y. x needs alpha-renaming, not lambda y. y.
- Explain deterministic fresh names and the free-variable preservation goal.

4:30--5:40 | Slide 6 -- Church encodings
- Explain the behavioral intuition: booleans choose, numerals iterate, and pairs pass their components to a selector.
- Point out that target programs contain no native numbers, booleans, pairs, arithmetic, or recursion primitives.

5:40--6:40 | Slide 7 -- Arithmetic and predecessor
- Name the closed library combinators and explain truncated subtraction as repeated predecessor.
- Give the intuition for Kleene predecessor: the continuation chain delays one successor application, while the zero path returns the base value.

6:40--7:40 | Slide 8 -- Compositional compiler rules
- Read only representative rules: variables, lambdas, application, let, pair, and fix.
- Explain that a full validation walk rejects unsupported constructs even in unselected branches, and compilation never calls the evaluator.

7:40--8:50 | Slide 9 -- Delayed control flow
- Explain why ordinary CBV would evaluate both conditional branches too soon.
- Walk through the thunk form: the boolean selects a lambda, and applying I forces only that selected lambda.
- Relate the same technique to short-circuit and/or.

8:50--9:50 | Slide 10 -- Recursion under CBV
- Explain why ordinary Y diverges under CBV and why Z adds a delaying lambda.
- Connect the fix translation to the factorial and Fibonacci examples you will show in the demo.

9:50--10:40 | Slide 11 -- Observing Church results
- Explain why weak values cannot be decoded merely by looking at syntax.
- Describe numeral spines, boolean markers, pair projection, and independent execution versus observation budgets.

10:40--11:30 | Slide 12 -- Testing strategy
- State the scope of the 13 automated tests: substitution, CBV order and budgets, encodings, scope, validation, and compiler purity.
- State the 14 pipeline demonstrations and distinguish tests from a proof.
